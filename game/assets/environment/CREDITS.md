### What was taken, and what was left

**Superseded.** This section used to record that only Color and Roughness
were imported, at 512px with mipmaps off, because no admissible visual
capture existed and normal maps could not be shown to buy anything.
`ARCHITECTURE.md`'s amended renderer rule retired that premise.

The current, authoritative record of what is imported and why lives beside
the files, in `materials/CREDITS.md`. In short: 1K PNG, Color + NormalGL +
Roughness + AmbientOcclusion (+ Metalness where shipped), mipmaps on, no
displacement. `core/materials/material_library.gd` resolves all of it.

The albedo textures are still used as *variance* on top of a value the tint
carries, so `tools/refs/measure_frame.py`'s luminance relationships survive.
The one exception is documented there as `preserve_albedo_mean`.

## Props

### `props/MetalFoldingChair.fbx` — PROVENANCE UNVERIFIED

The ringside folding chair, instanced 470 times by
`core/arena/arena_builder.gd`. 1448 triangles, one material, no textures.

**This asset arrived with no licence file, no author, and no source URL**, and
it is recorded that way rather than quietly filed under the CC0 heading above.
It was supplied by the project owner, who was shown
`gauntlet/anchor/ARCHITECTURE.md`'s rule on exactly this case --

> "Free to download" is not a licence. Extracted game assets and replicas of a
> real promotion's trade dress circulate freely and are still infringing;
> availability is not the test, the licence is.

-- and decided to use it anyway. That is their call on their own repository;
this entry exists so the decision is visible to anyone who reads the tree
later, and so nobody mistakes this file for one of the CC0 scans above.

What is known about it: the FBX is a Blender export whose embedded source path
is `E:\Applications\Blender\Models\Props\MetalFoldingChair\MetalFoldingChair.blend`,
which suggests a personal model library rather than a scrape. It is a generic
folding chair and carries no promotion's trade dress, so only its licence is
in question, not what it depicts.

If provenance is ever established, replace this section with a proper entry.
If it is established as unusable, `arena_builder.gd`'s chair can be rebuilt
from `_add_box()` primitives the way `_spectator_mesh()` is; nothing else
depends on the file.

## `arena_bowl.glb` — ORIGINAL, built in this repo

The rink and the seating bowl around it: the decked-over ice with its dasher
boards and cap rail, two tiers of seating with ~6,700 individually modelled
seats, the concourse, the suite storey with its LED ribbon boards and glass,
the aisle stair nosings, the obround outer wall and the roof. 101,928
triangles, ten named objects, one placeholder material each.

The whole thing is dimensioned from a regulation 60.96 x 25.91m sheet of ice
with 8.53m corners — a rink is its own corner radius offset from a rectangle,
so one rectangle generates the boards, every row of seating and the shell.

**No third party is involved in this file.** It is generated by
`tools/blender/arena_bowl.py`, which is committed, from constants that live in
`game/core/arena/arena_builder.gd` and are read out of it at build time. It
carries no scanned texture, no downloaded mesh and no promotion's trade dress
— the materials on it in the game are the same ambientCG CC0 sets listed
above, applied in Godot by `core/materials/material_library.gd`, and the
colours baked into the .glb are placeholders nothing in a frame ever uses.

The arena it is *shaped* like is recorded in `gauntlet/refs/arena.md`, under
the same reference-only standing as `refs/ring.md` and `refs/stage.md`: the
photographs there are measurement material, and nothing from them enters the
build as an asset.

Rebuild it with `tools/blender/build_arena.sh` (needs a Blender 4.x, or the
`bpy` module in a Python 3.11 environment). It is committed rather than built
in CI, and nothing in the game's build or test path requires Blender.

### Why the rest of the arena is generated, not imported

The textures here are generic CC0 surface scans, and the arena's set — ring,
ramp, stage, video wall, portals, truss, floor, barricades, ringside chairs
— is
generated by `game/core/arena/arena_builder.gd` from primitives rather than
imported as a third-party arena model. (The hall is empty: the crowd of box
impostors that used to sit in the bowl was removed, and the model's own seats
are the seating.) That is deliberate:

- The ring's own dimensions are load-bearing for measurement: `camera.md`
  derives the 41° FOV from the ropes sitting at 3.1 m, `match_camera.gd` picks
  its 1.45 m eye height by unprojecting those rope heights, and
  `test_camera_framing.gd` and `grapple_rig.gd` pin constants to the 6 m mat.
  Third-party ring geometry would invalidate that chain.

## ring_apron_banner.png — THIRD-PARTY MARKS, supplied by the project owner

The ring apron artwork (`materials/ring_apron_banner.png`, 1929x544) is
applied to all four apron sides (`scenes/ring.tscn`,
`StandardMaterial3D_apron`).

It carries **third-party trademarks** — the AEW *Dynamite* marks — and ships
with no licence, no author and no provenance, exactly like the folding chair
recorded above. `ARCHITECTURE.md` permits third-party assets without limit, so
it is in the build by that rule rather than by an exception.

**It now has a build path.** The banner used to be an opaque image supplied
directly by the project owner, which was fine for provenance and useless for
changing it: the crop, the perspective and where the wordmark sat on the skirt
were all unrecorded, so the only way to adjust any of it was to re-source the
art. `tools/textures/apron_banner.py` rectifies it out of one of the owner's
*Dynamite* reference photographs — a homography from four corners read off a
gridded overlay, then composited centred on a field taken from the banner's
own cloth.

The photograph itself is **not committed**, following the same convention as
every other reference here (`gauntlet/raw/README.md`, `gauntlet/refs/ring.md`):
measurements and observations are committable, the source is not. So the
script cannot run in CI and is not meant to — it is run by hand when the apron
changes, exactly like `tools/blender/*.py`.

Recorded here rather than left undeclared: it is fine for a prototype and for
internal capture, and it is **not cleared for distribution**. Anything shipped
publicly needs this replaced with original or licensed artwork. That is the
owner's decision to make; this file only makes sure nobody has to discover the
marks by looking at a screenshot.


## Entrance-set video — PROVENANCE UNVERIFIED

`video/dynamite_tron.ogv` and `video/dynamite_tron_still.png` — the graphics
package that loops on the entrance video wall, and one frame of it.

**This clip is third-party broadcast material.** It was supplied by the
project owner, who is aware of `gauntlet/anchor/ARCHITECTURE.md`'s rule on
third-party assets and of the fact that it carries a real promotion's trade
dress -- the marks in it are not this project's. It is recorded here rather
than filed under a licence it does not have, for the same reason the folding
chair above is: so the decision is visible to anyone reading the tree later.

The README's opening claim is that the game "ships fully original wrestlers,
movesets, and branding". This asset, like `ring_apron_banner.png`, is a
standing exception to that sentence and not covered by it.

Source: an MP4 supplied by the project owner. Transcoded to Ogg Theora --
Godot's built-in video backend plays Theora and nothing else -- by
`tools/assets/build_stage_video.sh`, which is committed so the conversion is
reproducible from the source. 1280x720, 30fps, 77.9s, no audio track, quality
4, 23MB.

`gauntlet/refs/stage.md` records the photographs of the set this clip belongs
to, and the same reference-only standing applies to them.
