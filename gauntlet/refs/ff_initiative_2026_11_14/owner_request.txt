AEGIS — Complete AEW Fight Forever Gameplay and Animation Improvement Initiative
Mission
I want you to significantly improve my Aegis wrestling game, using the reconstructed AEW Fight Forever Unreal Engine project as an architectural and gameplay reference.
AEW reference repository: https://github.com/peek6/AewFFCustomUProj
My Aegis repository: https://github.com/denhamd2/aegis
Your objective is to implement all the improvements described below, taking Aegis towards AAA-quality wrestling animations, believable physical interactions, more intelligent gameplay and authentic broadcast presentation.
Do not simply create a plan and stop. Execute the improvements incrementally, validate them, and continue through the work.
Quality is the primary objective. Token and execution efficiency are secondary.

Before making changes:
Read the latest AGENTS.md, CLAUDE.md, gauntlet/anchor/ARCHITECTURE.md, current prioritised backlog and relevant animation/quality documentation.
Inspect the AEW Fight Forever repository, particularly its gameplay architecture and animation interfaces.
Examine Aegis's existing systems, implemented moves, known defects and test coverage.
Identify which recommendations are already implemented, partially implemented or missing.
Establish the actual current baseline, rather than trusting historical progress reports.
Create a prioritised implementation roadmap integrated with the existing Aegis backlog.

2. Priority One — Grapple synchronisation and realistic physical interactions
Study the AEW synchronised animation architecture and improve Aegis's existing GrappleRig, PairedFit, PairedContacts, FootPlant and associated animation systems.
Implement:
Reliable synchronisation of attacker and defender animations.
Shared animation origins and correct character alignment.
Smooth entry into and exit from grapples.
Realistic grip and hand placement.
Consistent body contact throughout moves.
Improved foot planting, balance and weight transfer.
Better lifting, carrying and throwing mechanics.
Weight-class and body-proportion-aware animation adjustments.
Physically believable defender reactions and impact selling.
Smooth transitions between AnimationTree states, authored clips and cosmetic physics.
Elimination of obvious sliding, floating, clipping, snapping and interpenetration.
The wrestlers should look like they are genuinely interacting with and exerting force on each other, not performing two independent animations.
Preserve deterministic gameplay and keep cosmetic physics from changing authoritative match state.
3. Priority Two — Upgrade existing wrestling animations
Audit every currently implemented Fight Forever-inspired move.
Focus initially on:
One-Winged Angel
Tiger Driver '98
V-Trigger variations
Snap Dragon Suplexes
Cross Rhodes
Chokeslam
Spinebuster
Powerbomb variations
Samoan Drop
Spear
Buckshot Lariat and other configured signatures and finishers
Use the existing Fight Forever reference videos, frame measurements and QA documentation.
For every move:
Compare the Aegis animation with available reference footage.
Identify incorrect poses, movement trajectories, timing and contact.
Improve both attacker and defender animations in Blender.
Correct acceleration, deceleration, weight transfer and follow-through.
Ensure clean transitions into and out of the animation.
Add convincing impact reactions and natural recovery.
Check the animation from front, side and broadcast-camera perspectives.
Re-render, evaluate and refine until it meets the highest defensible quality standard.
Pay particular attention to the One-Winged Angel, whose previously recorded impact timing was slower than the measured reference.
All authored animations must retain the project's 60 FPS requirement.
Do not falsely label an animation AAA-quality or 10/10 simply because automated tests pass.
4. Priority Three — Rebuild a comprehensive reversal system
Investigate why the previous Aegis paired reversal system was removed before restoring any functionality.
Use Fight Forever's distinct reversal categories as design inspiration.
Implement:
Separate strike and grapple reversals.
Context-sensitive directional counters.
Special-move reversals.
Appropriate reversal timing windows.
Reversal opportunities based on the actual move and animation phase.
Properly synchronised attacker and defender counter animations.
Distinct successful and failed reversal reactions.
Reversal probabilities influenced by difficulty, stamina, damage and wrestler attributes.
Smooth transitions from counter animations into normal gameplay.
Where appropriate, use the existing reversal-window data in MoveDef.
Build the actual reversal animations and validate their gameplay integration. Do not merely add input bindings or unused states.
Reversals should be responsive, readable, balanced and visually convincing.
5. Priority Four — Improve wrestler AI and match psychology
Upgrade WrestlerAI, MatchFlow, MovePlaybook and related systems.
The goal is to make AI-versus-AI matches resemble believable televised wrestling matches.
Implement:
More intelligent move selection.
Distinct wrestler-specific fighting styles.
Realistic sequences of strikes, grapples and transitions.
Reduced move repetition.
Appropriate use of signatures and finishers.
Match phases including early offence, sustained control, comebacks, near-falls and finishes.
Better situational awareness of ropes, corners, apron, ringside and opponent position.
Strategic use of counters, dodges, submissions and recovery.
Appropriate stamina and momentum management.
More realistic use of taunts and special-move opportunities.
Weight-class-sensitive move decisions.
More varied matches without excessive random behaviour.
Wrestlers must retain their identities.
Kenny Omega should wrestle differently from Roman Reigns or Cody Rhodes.
Extend the existing data-driven systems instead of creating parallel or competing AI architectures.
Preserve existing match rules and owner decisions unless explicitly changed and documented.
6. Priority Five — Improve referee behaviour and near-falls
Use Fight Forever's referee animation structures as references.
Enhance:
Referee positioning during pinfalls.
Natural movement into counting position.
Start, loop, count and release animation phases.
Clear one-count, two-count and three-count actions.
Dramatic but believable near-falls.
Referee reactions to kickouts.
Rope-break detection and presentation.
Submission checks.
Match-ending and post-match transitions.
Collision avoidance around both wrestlers.
Keep referee timing aligned with Aegis's existing measured count schedule unless new evidence justifies changing it.
Improve the visual presentation without compromising deterministic pinfall and submission logic.
7. Priority Six — Expand the wrestling animation library
Aegis previously catalogued 146 Fight Forever moves, with 27 built or mapped and 119 outstanding. These are historical figures: re-audit the latest repository before proceeding.
Once the existing moves reach acceptable quality, progressively complete the remaining library.
Use an efficient reusable animation architecture:
High-quality base animations for move families.
Genuine variations in choreography, grips, timing and landing.
Shared animations across wrestlers where appropriate.
Unique animations for distinctive signatures and finishers.
Context-aware selection based on opponent position.
Properly authored attacker and defender animation pairs.
Move metadata, damage, momentum and reversal properties.
Cover strikes, grapples, suplexes, powerbombs, slams, submissions, aerial attacks, corner moves, rope moves, finishers and other catalogued families.
Avoid producing hundreds of nearly identical, low-quality animations to inflate completion statistics.
Only mark a move complete when it is playable, properly animated and validated.
8. Priority Seven — Broadcast-quality presentation
Improve match presentation using the existing camera, audio and impact systems.
Implement or refine:
Smooth broadcast camera tracking.
Context-sensitive grapple and finisher close-ups.
Correctly timed camera cuts around impacts.
Convincing impact audio and wrestler reactions.
Subtle camera shake where appropriate.
Natural character movement after impacts.
Readable near-fall and submission moments.
Cinematic but responsive finisher presentation.
Your previous Fight Forever analysis found that dramatic impact cuts are more important to its style than globally freezing the action.
Preserve smooth playback and avoid unnecessary whole-frame hit-stop.
9. Technical constraints
Aegis must remain a Godot-based game.
Preserve the repository's current engine version, architecture contract and existing Blender-to-Godot pipeline.
Requirements:
Godot 4, Forward+ and the repository's current physics configuration.
Fixed 60 Hz gameplay simulation.
60 FPS authored animations.
Target 60 FPS on MacBook M1 with 16 GB RAM.
Deterministic gameplay and replay compatibility.
Existing WrestlerFSM authority.
Existing GrappleRig ownership of paired moves.
Data-driven MoveDef configuration.
Existing automated testing and capture infrastructure.
Reproducible Blender generation and exports.
No unnecessary engine migration.
No paid external services outside the already approved subscriptions.
Avoid breaking existing character models, entrances, arena assets, match modes, weapons or visual improvements.
Follow the repository's development and publication permissions.
10. Implementation strategy and model routing
Use the existing Aegis agent architecture and model-routing rules.
Use the default agent for routine coding and integration. Delegate complex architecture and choreography decisions to the configured specialist, and Blender-specific work to the existing Blender specialist.
Use the configured visual reviewer selectively for rendered animation evidence.
Do not introduce additional paid models, tools or subscriptions without approval.
Work in manageable, focused batches.
For each batch:
Inspect the relevant existing implementation.
Define measurable acceptance criteria.
Make the required code and animation changes.
Execute focused tests.
Bake and inspect the actual animation or gameplay output.
Fix any quality or functionality regressions.
Update the quality ledger and backlog.
Commit, publish and deploy only in accordance with the repository's approved workflow.
Continue to the next improvement.
Do not repeatedly rebuild the entire animation library for minor changes when scoped tests can validate the work.
Do not allow automated tests to substitute for actual visual inspection.
11. Quality assurance requirements
Every substantial animation change must pass:
Animation timing and cadence tests.
Attacker/defender synchronisation checks.
Grip and contact-distance validation.
Collision and clipping checks.
Pose and deformation checks.
Transition smoothness checks.
Deterministic gameplay tests.
Relevant match-flow and move-selection tests.
Visual comparison with available reference footage.
Evaluate animation quality across eight dimensions:
Animation smoothness
Pose accuracy
Physical realism
Weight and impact
Attacker choreography
Defender selling
Timing and speed
Overall presentation
Use evidence-based scoring. Identify unresolved defects rather than inventing perfect scores.
Validate final performance on the target Mac hardware when available, and clearly distinguish verified results from pending native visual or performance acceptance.
12. Execution instructions
I approve the implementation of this improvement initiative.
This is an execution task, not a request for another research document.
Begin by reviewing the latest state of both repositories and identifying the most valuable first implementation batch.
Then start implementing the changes.
Do not replace existing working systems merely for stylistic consistency.
Do not make speculative architectural changes without establishing their benefit.
Respect repository rules requiring separate authorisation for commits, pushes or releases. Continue with independent work where possible rather than stopping the entire initiative.
If a recommendation is already implemented, validate its quality and move on.
If a feature requires significant additional development, break it into smaller deliverable batches while preserving the overall goal.
Keep a durable record of completed work, remaining tasks, test evidence and dependencies so the initiative can continue reliably across sessions.
13. Communication
I do not want long technical logs, code dumps or detailed reasoning in the chat.
Provide short, plain-English updates using:
Completed: What was actually improved.
Validated: Tests and visual checks that passed.
Remaining: Important unfinished work or quality gaps.
Next: The next implementation batch.
Do not claim success before verifying the result.
Start now
Begin with a current-state audit, then implement the highest-impact improvements to wrestler synchronisation, grappling and animation quality.
The long-term goal is a polished, technically robust wrestling game with the responsiveness of Fight Forever, more realistic character interactions, excellent animation quality and convincing televised-match presentation.
Build the improvements, test them, refine them and progressively complete the initiative.
