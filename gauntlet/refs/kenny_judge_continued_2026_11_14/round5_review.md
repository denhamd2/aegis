## Findings

- The forelock no longer projects as far forward like a rigid handle. This
  is a visible local improvement.
- However, the crown and back now expose a large, almost featureless brown
  cap. This is especially clear in `head_back` and `hair_top`; sparse clumps
  and a few dark/pale strands do not cover the helmet-like surface. The
  primary references instead show dense overlapping curl masses, dark roots
  and fine irregular transitions into blonde strands.
- Eyebrow pigment reads as two thin tracks on the forehead above the orbital
  shelf, rather than brows following the visible ridge. The heavy angular
  upper-face mass still produces an exaggerated scowl and bulging-eye read.
  These features remain a poor match to Kenny's reference eye/brow structure.
- Body proportions remain broadly athletic and essentially unchanged. The
  coarse skin/beard and stretched shoulder/pectoral surface detail continue
  to limit realism. The lower material score primarily reflects the newly
  flat, exposed hair-cap presentation, not a required change in photo colour
  cast or sweat level.

## Highest-value round 6 targets

1. Recover a fully covered crown/back foundation before adding more detail.
   The cleaner layered mass from published round 3 is a minimum visual
   baseline if needed. Hide the cap under overlapping rounded curl clumps;
   a dark vertex-colour cap alone is not a finished visible hair surface.
2. Place eyebrow pigment on the actual orbital ridge in the final runtime
   pose and soften the exaggerated shelf. Diagnose the final visible result
   rather than accepting source-space placement as proof of correct fit.
3. Retain the closer forelock while adding fine curl/strand breakup and
   dark-root/blonde-end transitions. No broad body changes are justified.

The authorised continued sequence is rounds 4–6. This review requests no
additional round beyond that scope and promises no score increase. Static
