# Art-director review — completed move sheets (8 November 2026)

Reviewer: DeepSeek V4 Pro, acting as art director. Pro has no image input, so
the pass used the frame-by-frame evidence from the free visual-QA reviewer
(`visual-review-owa-samoan.md`, `visual-review-chokeslam.md`) and the project
animation bars. Sheets are seven-beat side/close captures at 480×270; the
match-camera view was not available for this pass.

## Scores

| Move | Realism /10 | Animation quality /10 | Overall /10 | AAA bar |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| One Winged Angel | 2.5 | 3.0 | 2.5 | No |
| Squatting Samoan Drop | 3.0 | 3.5 | 3.0 | No |
| Chokeslam | 2.5 | 3.0 | 2.5 | No |

All three are structurally identifiable, but none passes the AAA bar. The
Samoan Drop is the closest of the three.

## Common failures

1. **No loaded lift.** The attacker stays bolt-upright with planted feet while
   holding a full body, with no counter-lean, knee flexion, step or bracing.
   The defender reads as a rigid plank. Both moves look weightless.
2. **The impact frame is missing.** In every sheet the collision happens
   between two beats; there is no contact frame with mat compression, recoil or
   hit-stop.
3. **The landing has no sell.** The defender settles perfectly flat and
   motionless; the attacker recovers immediately with no exertion cue.
4. **The grip is not readable.** Hands, fingers and contact points are too
   small to verify at 480×270; the defender can read as held by an invisible
   constraint. A larger close-up pass is required before sign-off.

## One Winged Angel — 2.5 / 3.0 / 2.5

- The carry is a weightless horizontal plank; the attacker's head and the
  defender's torso merge at 0.50.
- The sit-out at 0.65, the move's signature, is the least readable beat: the
  rotation driver is invisible and the defender's arms flail.
- The impact is skipped between 0.65 and 0.80.

Top fixes: loaded carry (lean, knee flexion, step, head clearance); a distinct
impact frame with compression and recoil; a larger grip/flip close-up with the
legs driven by the hold and the arms following inertially.

## Squatting Samoan Drop — 3.0 / 3.5 / 3.0

- The fireman's-rack contact is distributed across the shoulders, which reads
  better than the OWA's two-point plank, and the drop rotation is simpler.
- The attacker still stands upright under the load; the defender's back likely
  overlaps the attacker's head at 0.50.
- The drop reads as a pose change into kneeling, and the impact is again
  between beats.

Top fixes: load response and head clearance at the rack; a hip/arm-driven drop
with an impact frame; a defended landing sell and delayed attacker recovery.

## Chokeslam — 2.5 / 3.0 / 2.5

- The 0.50 peak silhouette genuinely reads as a chokeslam; it is the move's one
  strong frame.
- The one-handed throat grip cannot be confirmed, the lift has no arc or
  pull-in, and the attacker shows no load.
- The drop collapses into one beat; the impact frame is absent and the landing
  is a symmetrical ragdoll.

Top fixes: a braced, loaded one-handed lift with a readable throat grip; a
three-beat drop with a contact frame and mat compression; an asymmetric landing
sprawl and an attacker exertion sell.

## Highest-leverage next pass

Build one shared **loaded lift + impact + sell** template and apply it across
the moveset:

- attacker braces and shifts centre of mass under the load;
- defender sags at the contact points;
- a dedicated contact frame with mat compression and one rebound;
- at least two sell beats with an asymmetric settle;
- a delayed attacker recovery with a visible effort cue.

The impact/sell layer is the common failure in all three sheets, but it must
land with the loaded-lift layer: an impact pop on a weightless setup would only
read as a camera trick.
