# No Holds Barred and its weapons -- research, decisions, conventions

The owner's brief: a mode select on the select screen -- **1 ON 1** (today's
match) and **NO HOLDS BARRED** -- with lots of weapons, most of them from
under the ring, all AAA. Decided with the owner: firearms are literal (a
handgun and an RPG), the weapon wheel holds far more than 2K's six, and
fighting outside the ring is in scope.

## Research (WWE 2K26 and the real thing)

- No Holds Barred in 2K26 is one on one only: "anything goes, no
  disqualifications"; pinfall or submission, no DQ, no count-out
  (thesmackdownhotel.com/wwe2k26/match-types, thegamer.com).
- Weapons come from under the ring: stand at the middle of an apron side,
  press pick-up, choose from a menu (the Weapon Wheel, 6 customisable slots
  in 2K). Held: strike, secondary strike, throw (direction + button), drop
  (games.gg, realsport101.com).
- 2K26 pool: chair, kendo stick, ladder, table, sledgehammer, baseball bat,
  barbed-wire bat, stop sign, hockey stick, shovel, trash can and lid,
  guitar, microphone, megaphone, light tube, door, safety cone, chancla,
  plus new thumbtacks (pour on the mat or swing the bag), shopping cart,
  barbed-wire chair, bed of nails, drum, beer can; stackable tables; the
  championship belt on a ringside podium (Smackdown Hotel, Dexerto).
- Fire extinguisher: backstage-only in 2K; in the ring the classic spot is
  the spray that blinds (Cena on Laurinaitis, Bob Orton in a casket match).
- The famous wrestling gun: Austin's on McMahon, Raw, 19 Oct 1998.
- Signature weapons (WWE.com): Foley's barbed-wire bat "Barbie", Triple H's
  sledgehammer, Sandman's kendo stick, Edge & Christian's Con-Chair-To,
  Regal's brass knuckles, Dusty's bull rope, Honky Tonk Man's guitar.

## The weapons, by animation family

A family shares one set of carry, strike and throw clips; each weapon adds
only what is its own. That is what makes this many weapons AAA in scope.

| # | Family | Weapons |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Chairs | steel folding chair, barbed-wire chair |
| 2 | Swung | baseball bat, barbed-wire bat, kendo stick, sledgehammer, 2x4 with nails, lead pipe, hockey stick, stop sign, guitar, crutch, shovel, inflatable banana |
| 3 | Carried and slammed | trash can and lid, steel steps, ring bell, championship belt, cookie sheet, frying pan, briefcase |
| 4 | Set pieces | table (stackable, breaks), ladder, thumbtacks (bag + scattered), bed of nails, light-tube bundle, barbed-wire board |
| 5 | Sprays and firearms | fire extinguisher, handgun, RPG (launcher, rocket, its blast) |
| 6 | Whips and fists | chain, weight belt (Cody's), brass knuckles |

Per man: Roman -- slow, deliberate chair shots in threes, the Spear through
a table or the barricade. Cody -- the weight-belt whip, Cross Rhodes onto a
chair, the Cody Cutter off a ladder. Kenny -- V-Trigger into a chair held to
the face, a dive through a table.

## Modelling conventions (wave 1)

Every weapon is an original design -- no 2K or other game assets, nothing
downloaded without a licence recorded in game/assets/weapons/CREDITS.md.

- **Script**: `tools/blender/weapons/<family>.py`, one per family, building
  every weapon in it. Run headless with the repo's bpy
  (`python3 tools/blender/weapons/<family>.py`); import `venue` for bpy, as
  the other tools/blender scripts do. **Deterministic**: the same script
  gives byte-identical .glb and textures (no time, no unseeded randomness;
  seed any noise from the weapon's name).
- **Output**: `game/assets/weapons/<weapon_id>.glb`, one file per weapon,
  textures embedded (or `game/assets/weapons/textures/<weapon_id>_*.png`,
  also generated by the script). weapon_id is snake_case: steel_chair,
  barbed_wire_bat, fire_extinguisher, rpg, ...
- **Axes and scale**: the game's -- +Y up, forward -Z, metres, real-world
  sizes (a folding chair seat at 0.45 m, a bat 0.84 m, a table 1.83 x 0.76 m
  at 0.74 m high).
- **Origin**: hand-held weapons at the primary grip, where the right hand
  closes; set pieces (table, ladder, steps, bed of nails) at floor centre.
- **Markers** (empties, exported as nodes, named exactly):
  - `grip_r` -- the right hand's grip; its +Y runs along the handle toward
    the striking end, +Z out of the back of the hand;
  - `grip_l` -- the left hand's, for anything held two-handed;
  - `strike` -- the centre of the striking surface (the barrel of a bat,
    the chair's seat edge, the can's base);
  - `muzzle` -- extinguisher nozzle, gun muzzle, launcher tube mouth, +Z out;
  - set pieces add their own: `top` (a table's surface centre), `rung_N`
    (ladder rungs), `seat` (chair seat centre).
- **States**: one root node per state, named `<WeaponId>_<State>`:
  - every weapon: `_Clean`;
  - wear: `_Dented` / `_Bent` (chair, can, sign, cookie sheet, pan);
  - break: `_Broken` as pre-fractured separate meshes, each a child named
    `piece_NN` with its origin at its own centre, laid out in place
    (table, guitar, light tubes, 2x4, kendo stick, inflatable banana
    deflated);
  - configurations: chair `_Folded` / `_Open`, table `_Folded` / `_Up`,
    ladder `_Closed` / `_Open`, thumbtacks `_Bag` / `_Scattered`;
  - firearms: the RPG's `Rpg_Rocket` and `Rpg_Blast` (smoke and flash
    shells), the gun's `Handgun_Flash`; the extinguisher's `_Cloud`.
- **Triangles** (.claude/skills/references/polycount-budgets.md): hand-held
  3k-15k; set pieces up to 20k; the pieces of a broken state about the
  whole's count.
- **Materials**: glTF PBR from Principled BSDF -- base colour, normal,
  occlusion-roughness-metal, 1024-2048 px, generated procedurally in the
  script. This is where AAA lives: chipped paint and scuffs on a steel
  chair, dents on a can, grain and splinters in a table, tape on a bat's
  handle, wire with a real twist, the banana's foam sheen, gunmetal and
  wear on the gun. Real-world-calibrated values (steel metallic 1.0,
  painted steel 0, roughness from the reference, albedo not over 0.85).
  Material names `<weapon_id>_<part>`.
- **Review**: render each state in the game's renderer, on the bare floor
  beside a mannequin for scale:

      xvfb-run -a godot4 --path game --rendering-driver vulkan \
          --resolution 1280x720 tools/probe/weapon_shot.tscn -- \
          --glb res://assets/weapons/<id>.glb --node <State> --out <dir> [--lift 1.0]

  (`godot4 --headless --import --path game` first, so the new .glb is
  imported). Look at every frame; fix what reads wrong; then a contact sheet
  per weapon goes in the hand-back.
